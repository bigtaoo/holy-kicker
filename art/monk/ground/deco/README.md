# Ground decorations (art item 4, 2026-10-01)

The grass tile repeats every 768 world units (512 px at 1.5 units per px), so its white
flowers line up on a visible grid. Two layers break it up, switched with `?deco=none|patches|props`
(default `props`):

- **Patches**: a soft white disc from `tools/pack_deco.py`, tinted dark green (alpha 0.38) or
  light yellow-green (0.16), 1100-2200 units across on a 1000-unit jittered grid. They break up
  the larger-scale lighting so the tile period stops reading; they cost no art.
- **Props**: six images generated by text-to-image from the `*.txt` prompts here: grass tuft,
  bush, rocks, dirt patch, stump, gravestone. Each 300-unit grid cell gets one prop with a
  60 % chance; tufts are the most common. They are tinted down a little, and the dirt patch is
  drawn at 70 % alpha, so mobs and gems stay the brightest things on the grass.

Findings:
- Text-to-image draws these props more painterly than the sticker figures (thin outlines,
  more detail). On the ground that helps: they sit behind the figures instead of competing with them.
- The first gravestone was light grey stone on white. Cutout flooded into it through the
  cracks and left only the outline. Asking for "dark slate grey-blue stone" fixed it. Rule:
  props must be clearly darker than the white background.
- Even with 300 mobs packed around the hero, the props read as ground. None of them is
  mistaken for an enemy or a pickup.
- Cost: about 490 static sprites from one 512x406 sheet (240 KB), one container under all
  the figures, drawn in one batch with no depth sorting. Props are not depth sorted against
  mobs, so a mob walks over a stump or gravestone; that is fine for low props, and tall props
  (trees, walls) would need sorting.
